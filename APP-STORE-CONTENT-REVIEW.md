# Content review, for age rating

What is actually in the game, checked against the App Store rating questions.
The rating itself is yours to select — this is the evidence for it.

## What the game is

A turn-based strategy game about a fictional election across the 117 real
Punjab Assembly constituencies. Players found invented parties and spend a
campaign budget across seats over twenty rounds.

## Simulated gambling — worth thinking about

The game has a "Corruption / bribe" campaign action with a weighted random
outcome table: it can pay off, do nothing, or trigger an investigation and a
fine. There is a "gambler" strategy in the balance tests.

**It is not gambling.** There is no wager of real money or anything of value,
no purchase of chances, no payout, and no real currency anywhere in the game.
The in-game money is a campaign budget that cannot be bought or cashed out.

Random outcomes on an action are ordinary strategy-game mechanics. But the
action is *named* corruption and bribery, which reviewers read as a theme
rather than a mechanic — see the next section.

## User-generated content — this is the one that matters

Players type:

- **A party name** (up to 40 characters)
- **A short name / abbreviation** (up to 4 letters)
- **A slogan** (optional)

These are visible to the other three players in a multiplayer game.

**There is currently no profanity filter, no reporting of names, and no
moderation queue.** The game does have a rival-reporting mechanic, but it is a
gameplay action about in-game corruption, not a content report.

Apple's guideline 1.2 expects apps with user-generated content to have a way
to filter objectionable content, a way to report it, and a way to block abusive
users. **[NEEDS A DECISION]** — the realistic options are:

1. Add a filter on party name, short name and slogan, plus a report control.
2. Restrict names to a fixed list players choose from.
3. Make multiplayer names visible only within a private game code — weaker, and
   probably not sufficient on its own.

## Political content

The game is about elections in a real place, using real constituency names. It
does not:

- take a political position
- endorse or criticise any party or person
- present results as real or predictive
- claim any official status

It does currently ship **portraits of real politicians and real party
symbols** — see `CONTENT-RIGHTS-REVIEW.md`. That is a rights problem first and
a content problem second, and it also bears on how a reviewer reads the whole
app.
